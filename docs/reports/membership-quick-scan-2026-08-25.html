First-year retention collapsed for the two newest cohorts

Of households that joined in each fiscal year, the share still members one year later. Every cohort from 2010 to 2023 kept 84–91% through year one.

So what: the FY2024 cohort kept only 69% and FY2025 only 67% — roughly double the historical first-year loss. Whatever brought the 2024–25 wave in (introductory tiers, a campaign, a pricing change) is not converting to a second year. This is the single most important thing to investigate.

Joining for religious school is not stickier — it's below average

Households that joined in FY2014–FY2022 (so every one has had at least three full years) and recorded a reason for joining. Share still members today, by stated reason; a household counts under every reason it named.

School-driven joinsOther reasons

So what: across 1,400 recent joiners, households drawn in by clergy, Streicker, or family ties are the most durable (55–65%). Joining because of religious school retains 37% — below the 41% for a plain "affiliation" — and nursery school is the weakest entry point at 26%. The hypothesis that school families are stickier is not supported at the point of joining.

…but families whose children actually go through religious school do stay

Same FY2014–FY2022 joiners, grouped by whether the household ever had a child enrolled (any join reason). Single measure, so one color.

So what: households with a child who went through religious school retain 48% versus 40% with no school history — and families that did both nursery and religious school retain 68%. Nursery-school-only families are the flight risk (34%). The practical read: nursery school is the entry door and religious school is the retention engine; the leak is families who don't make the transition between them.

Why people leave: non-payment has become the #1 reason

Coded resignation reasons by fiscal year (reason coding is reliable from FY2021). Stacked so the height is total coded resignations.

So what: "non-payment" — quiet non-renewal rather than an explicit decision — went from 5 in FY2021 to 107 in FY2025 and 90 in FY2026. Together with the rising first-year losses above, this points at renewal friction and lapsed introductory memberships rather than people actively choosing to leave. Moves (~50–70/yr) and the young-adult tier aging out (~25–33/yr) are steady, structural losses.

What to do with this

Where the data points next — the questions worth building into the app as live, repeatable views:

  1. Diagnose the FY2024–25 first-year churn. Split those cohorts by tier (Young Professionals / Downtown introductory vs. Voting) and by join reason; the billing statements will show whether they were ever invoiced for year two.
  2. Track the nursery → religious school hand-off. Per-year enrollment (Class Enrollment object) makes "did this NS family enroll in RS?" and "how many years after the last RS year did they leave?" exact.
  3. Turn non-payment into an at-risk list. Households billed but unpaid this fiscal year, before they become next year's resignations.
  4. Watch the young-adult tier conversion. ~30 households/yr age out; how many convert to Voting membership vs. lapse?

Caveats: pre-2019 dates come from the legacy iMIS migration; 294 records with placeholder join dates were excluded; "still members" uses the current membership flag, so recent cohorts have had less time to churn (their year-1 figures are directly comparable, later years are not).
